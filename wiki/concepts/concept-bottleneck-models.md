# Concept Bottleneck Models

## Definition

Concept bottleneck models (CBMs) are architectures that route prediction through an
intermediate layer of human-interpretable concepts before producing a downstream
label, action, or generation. The promise is not only explanation, but also
intervention: a user should be able to inspect or edit concept values and change
the final outcome in predictable ways.

ICLR 2026 showed that this idea is no longer a single architecture family.
Poster papers explored whether the bottleneck is truly minimal, whether concepts
can be expressed in natural language, whether concept channels can steer
latent-diffusion models, and whether multi-level concept hierarchies are needed
for realistic reasoning. That makes CBMs a central bridge between
[Mechanistic Interpretability](mechanistic-interpretability.md) and
[Concept Representation Learning](concept-representation-learning.md).

A recurring theme across the 2026 work is that interpretability depends on what
information is allowed to pass through the concept layer. If concept variables
quietly carry extra task-relevant information, then interventions become harder
to trust even when concept prediction accuracy looks strong.

## Key Ideas

- **Concept supervision as interface**: CBMs make latent structure legible by
  aligning internal variables with named concepts rather than unnamed features.
- **Intervenability matters more than explanation alone**: a good concept layer
  should support meaningful edits, not just post hoc descriptions.
- **Leakage is the core fault line**: [There Was Never a Bottleneck in Concept
  Bottleneck Models](../papers/no-bottleneck-concept-bottleneck-models.md) argues
  that standard CBMs often encode more than the named concept.
- **Static or constrained embeddings help**: [An Information-Theoretic Approach
  to Benign Leakage](../papers/info-theoretic-benign-leakage.md) treats leakage
  as something to control rather than merely observe.
- **Concepts need not be scalar labels**: [Simulating Concept Bottlenecks with
  Vision-Language Models](../papers/simulating-concept-bottlenecks-vlm.md)
  uses textual concept descriptions generated by a VLM.
- **Generative models need concept-aware latents**: [Concept Bottleneck
  Diffusion](../papers/concept-bottleneck-diffusion.md) pushes bottlenecks into
  diffusion latents for steerable generation.
- **Real concepts are hierarchical**: [Digging Deeper](../papers/digging-deeper-concept-hierarchies.md)
  argues that flat concept sets miss useful multi-level abstractions.

## Related Work at ICLR 2026

- [There Was Never a Bottleneck in Concept Bottleneck Models](../papers/no-bottleneck-concept-bottleneck-models.md)
  introduces minimal CBMs and frames leakage as a foundational problem.
- [Simulating Concept Bottlenecks with Vision-Language Models](../papers/simulating-concept-bottlenecks-vlm.md)
  shows that natural-language concept descriptions can serve as the bottleneck.
- [Concept Bottleneck Diffusion for Steerable Generation](../papers/concept-bottleneck-diffusion.md)
  adapts the idea from classifiers to diffusion-based generation.
- [An Information-Theoretic Approach to Benign Leakage in Static Concept
  Embedding Models](../papers/info-theoretic-benign-leakage.md) proposes static
  concept embeddings with controlled information injection.
- [Digging Deeper: Learning Multi-Level Concept Hierarchies](../papers/digging-deeper-concept-hierarchies.md)
  extends concept models to hierarchical concepts and multi-level intervention.
- The [UniCRL workshop capture](../../raw/iclr-2026-workshop-unicrl-concept-representation.md)
  treats CBMs as one strand in a broader concept-learning agenda.

## Open Questions

- When does concept leakage destroy the reliability of concept-level edits?
- What is the right trade-off between strict bottlenecks and performance-preserving
  “benign” side channels?
- Can textual, visual, and causal concepts be unified in one intervention-ready
  interface?
- How should CBMs be evaluated when concepts are hierarchical, uncertain, or
  only partially observed?
