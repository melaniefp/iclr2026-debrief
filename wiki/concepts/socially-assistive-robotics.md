# Socially Assistive Robotics

Socially assistive robotics (SAR) is a subfield of robotics focused on creating robots that provide assistance through social interaction rather than physical task completion. The robot helps people help themselves — coaching, motivating, and supporting behavior change through embodied social engagement.

## Key ideas

- SAR robots use real-time perception (vision, speech, affect recognition) to understand users and adapt behavior accordingly
- The goal is **augmentation** of human capability, not replacement of human roles
- Physically co-present robots produce stronger engagement, enjoyment, learning, and retention than screen-based alternatives, supported by neuroscience evidence on embodied cognition and mirror neuron systems
- Low-cost, non-humanoid embodiments (sub-$300) are more scalable, less threatening, and create fewer unmet expectations than humanoid robots
- Personality matching between robot and user significantly improves outcomes (e.g., longer exercise duration in stroke patients)
- Real-world deployments span infant motor development, autism intervention, dementia screening, anxiety/depression support, and cancer survivor groups
