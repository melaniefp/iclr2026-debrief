# Model Collapse

## Definition

Model collapse is the progressive narrowing or corruption of a model's learned
distribution when training data are increasingly generated by models themselves
or otherwise filtered through a biased synthetic loop. The most visible symptom
is loss of diversity: models keep reproducing high-density, high-confidence
modes while rare but real structure disappears.

At ICLR 2026, collapse was discussed in two complementary ways. Richard
Baraniuk's Sci4DL talk framed it as a systems-level consequence of synthetic-data
feedback loops, while [Model Collapse Is Not a Bug but a Feature in Machine
Unlearning for LLMs](../papers/model-collapse-machine-unlearning.md) showed that
a controlled version of collapse can be deliberately induced to remove unwanted
information.

This makes model collapse more than a pathology label. It is both a warning about
recursive data ecosystems and a possible mechanism for targeted forgetting,
linking it closely to [Self-Consuming AI](self-consuming-ai.md).

## Key Ideas

- **Collapse is a distributional effect**: repeated training on biased synthetic
  samples suppresses tail events and shrinks diversity.
- **Mode-seeking sampling accelerates the problem**: if only “good-looking”
  outputs are kept, the next model sees an even narrower world.
- **Quality can fall even when samples look plausible**: collapse often hides in
  recall, coverage, and calibration before it becomes visually obvious.
- **Synthetic loops are becoming structural**: Baraniuk's talk argues that web
  saturation with generated content makes recursive exposure hard to avoid.
- **The collapse direction can be exploited**: the machine-unlearning paper uses
  partial model collapse as a privacy-preserving forgetting mechanism.
- **Monitoring collapse requires ecosystem thinking**: the unit of analysis is
  often the training loop, not just a single model checkpoint.

## Related Work at ICLR 2026

- [Model Collapse Is Not a Bug but a Feature in Machine Unlearning for LLMs](../papers/model-collapse-machine-unlearning.md)
  proposes Partial Model Collapse as an unlearning tool that avoids direct
  optimization on private targets.
- [Richard Baraniuk on Self-Consuming Loops](../articles/baraniuk-self-consuming-loops-talk.md)
  explains how synthetic feedback loops drive diversity loss and how NEON tries
  to reverse the bad update direction.
- [Summary: Scientific Methods for Understanding Deep Learning](../summaries/iclr-2026-workshop-sci4dl.md)
  places collapse inside a broader science-of-training-dynamics agenda.
- The raw [Sci4DL transcript](../../raw/iclr-2026-workshop-sci4dl/transcript.md)
  also raises open questions about cross-model contamination and human exposure
  to AI-generated “slop.”

## Open Questions

- How much synthetic data can a training pipeline tolerate before useful support
  begins to disappear?
- Can collapse be detected early enough to prevent irreversible diversity loss?
- When is collapse a safe unlearning mechanism rather than destructive drift?
- How should evaluation distinguish collapse from ordinary domain shift or
  over-optimization on benchmark-style data?
