# Human-Robot Interaction

Human-robot interaction (HRI) studies how people perceive, relate to, and collaborate with physically embodied robotic agents. It encompasses the design of robot behavior, form, and social capabilities to achieve effective and acceptable human-machine partnerships.

## Key ideas

- People relate to physically embodied agents differently than to screen-based interfaces — different brain regions activate, engagement is higher, and information retention is longer
- Robot embodiment creates expectations: animal-like robots are expected to behave like animals, humanoids are expected to match human capabilities, leading to frequent disappointment
- People often anthropomorphize robots, feeling empathy and relatedness, but also exhibit in-group/out-group dynamics including bullying and abuse
- Embodiment form should match the interaction context — abstract, non-humanoid forms can be preferable because they create fewer biased expectations
- Users disclose more personal information to robots they believe are autonomous versus human-controlled
- Therapist-client alignment (how productively a user engages with their interaction partner) is the strongest predictor of therapeutic outcomes, and physically embodied agents achieve stronger alignment than chatbots
