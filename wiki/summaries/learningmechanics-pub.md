# Summary: Learning Mechanics

**TL;DR:** Learning Mechanics is a theory-focused blog launched out of the Sci4DL orbit that tries to make deep-learning science more cumulative, visual, and experimentally grounded.

The site was introduced alongside Jeremy Cohen's Sci4DL talk and presents itself as a home for long-form explanations of training dynamics, scaling behavior, and scientific methodology for deep learning. The emphasis is not on announcing new benchmark wins, but on building shared conceptual tools for understanding why neural networks learn the way they do.

The initial posts illustrate that goal well. The deep-linear-networks essay uses toy models to explain staged learning, low-rank updates, and benign nonconvexity; the quanta essay revisits neural scaling laws through the lens of discrete skill emergence; and the “Perspectives” set argues for a slower, more cumulative scientific style. Across these posts, simple models and careful exposition are treated as valuable research artifacts in their own right.

That orientation makes the blog a useful complement to this repo's ICLR captures. It provides background context for the [Edge of Stability](../concepts/edge-of-stability.md) and [Neural Scaling Laws](../concepts/neural-scaling-laws.md) concept pages, and it resonates strongly with the more workshop-centered [edge-of-stability article](../articles/edge-of-stability-cohen-talk.md).

As a raw capture, Learning Mechanics is also a reminder that not all important sources are papers or conference pages. Some of the clearest articulation of the emerging “science of deep learning” agenda is happening in public explanatory writing like this.
