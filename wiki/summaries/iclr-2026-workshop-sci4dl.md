# Scientific Methods for Understanding Deep Learning (Sci4DL)

**TL;DR:** The Sci4DL workshop at ICLR 2026 promoted the use of the scientific method—forming hypotheses and designing controlled experiments—as a rigorous complement to mathematical theory for understanding deep learning.

The workshop was organized by **Zahra Kadkhodaie**, **Florentin Guth**, **Sanae Lotfi**, **Davis Brown**, **Antonio Sclocchi**, **Sharvaree Vadgama**, **James Simon**, and **Eero Simoncelli**. Their goal was to bridge the gap between the rapid empirical progress of deep learning and the slower pace of formal theoretical understanding, arguing that carefully designed experiments can yield actionable scientific insights even where proofs remain elusive.

The program featured five invited talks covering a wide range of perspectives on deep learning science: **Jeremy Cohen** on gradient descent dynamics, **Julia Kempe** on LLM reasoning capabilities, **David Bau** on mechanistic interpretability, **Richard Baraniuk** on self-consuming generative loops and model collapse, and **Matthieu Wyart** on neural scaling laws arising from natural language statistics. Three contributed talks complemented these with work on zeroth-order optimization at the edge of stability, data efficiency in training, and scaling laws for hierarchical models.

The day also included a panel discussion with all invited speakers, a presentation by winners of the Science of DL Challenge, and two poster sessions providing opportunities for broader community engagement and discussion.
