## Invited Talk: Karen E. Adolph (9:00 AM – 10:00 AM)

**Overflow Room: 201 A/B**

Behavior is everything we do. With age and experience, infant behavior becomes more flexible, adaptive, and functional. More intelligent. How do infants acquire intelligent behavior? Babies are learning while developing. Advances in motor skills expand infants' interactions with the environment. In the course of everyday activity, infants acquire immense amounts of time-distributed, variable, error-filled practice for every type of foundational behavior. Practice is largely spontaneous, self-motivated, and frequently not goal directed. Formal robot models suggest that infants' natural practice regimen — replete with variability and errors — is optimally suited for building an intelligent behavioral system. I propose that open video sharing will speed progress toward understanding behavior and its development.

**Bio:** Karen Adolph is Julius Silver Professor of Psychology and Neuroscience and Professor of Applied Psychology and Child and Adolescent Psychiatry at NYU. She uses observable motor behaviors to study developmental processes. She directs the Databrary.org video library and PLAY-project.org. She is an APA, APS, and AAAS Fellow and Past-President of ICIS.

## Invited Talk: Percy Liang (5:45 PM – 6:45 PM)

**Overflow Room: 201 A/B** — *Marin: Open Development of Frontier AI*

As AI capabilities skyrocket, openness plummets: the scientific community and broader public knows little of how frontier models (including open-weight models) are trained. I will describe Marin, a radically new way of doing model development, inspired by true open-source software. Every experiment is done in the open, and anyone can suggest ideas, review, and even run experiments through GitHub, providing a better way of doing science that improves on preregistration, reproducibility, and peer review. I will discuss a selection of scientific results that have emerged from Marin, including new optimizers and scaling laws.

**Bio:** Percy Liang is a Professor of Computer Science at Stanford University, co-founder of Together AI and Simile AI, and the creator of Marin. Contributions include SQuAD, HELM benchmarking framework, generative agents, prefix tuning, and coining "foundation models". Awards include PECASE (2019), IJCAI Computers and Thought Award (2016), NSF CAREER Award (2016), Sloan Fellowship (2015).

## Expo Talk Panels

### Morning (12:00 PM – 1:00 PM)

**Jump Trading: Foundation Models for Market Data**
Jump Trading processes terabytes of market data daily. Quantitative research workflows applying foundation models and multi-agent systems. Results from fine-tuning LLMs on textual input for live trading signals. Discussion of hallucination risk management, strict point-in-time correctness, and evaluation methodology.

**ParaRNN: Breaking the Sequence-Parallelization Barrier**
ParaRNN, a framework that breaks the sequence-parallelization barrier for nonlinear RNNs. Achieves speedups of up to 665x over naive sequential application. Successfully training 7B-parameter LSTM and GRU models with perplexity comparable to Transformers and Mamba2.

### Afternoon (12:45 PM – 1:45 PM)

**Shopify SimGym: Offline A/B Testing with AI Shoppers**
SimGym, a scalable system for rapid offline A/B testing using merchant-specific AI shoppers. Leverages per-merchant storefront logs from real shoppers. Accurately predicts direction and relative magnitude of treatment effect, reducing experiment iteration from weeks to under an hour.

**Prolific: AI Evaluation Methodology & Infrastructure**
Building evaluation methodology and infrastructure for frontier AI. Over 200,000 verified participants across 45 countries. Case studies: "Unpacking Human Preference for LLMs: HUMAINE Framework" and "Missing Red Line: How Commercial Pressure Erodes AI Safety Boundaries."
