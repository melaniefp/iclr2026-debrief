## Workshops

### 1. GenAI for Genomics

Generative AI is transforming biology, with breakthrough applications like directed evolution in protein science. The parallel ambition to engineer cellular and tissue states in genomics is a major frontier. Through a field-grounding keynote by a genomics expert, invited talks, contributed presentations, and a moderated debate, the workshop targets core challenges: data generation priorities, model design for genomic hierarchies, biologically-grounded evaluation frameworks, and interpretability.

### 2. Trustworthy AI by Design

Developing trustworthy AI systems by principled design: models that are interpretable, robust, and aligned across the full lifecycle. Six pillars: (1) Interpretable and Intervenable Models, (2) Inference-Time Safety and Monitoring, (3) Multimodal Trust Challenges, (4) Robustness and Threat Models, (5) Trust Evaluation and Responsible Deployment, (6) Safety and Trustworthiness in LLM Agents. Unifying efforts across modalities (language, vision, audio, time series) and technical areas.

### 3. Scientific Foundation Models

Problem-driven design: models that measurably advance real scientific inquiries — forecasting extreme climate events, accelerating materials discovery, understanding biological mechanisms. Scientific data are physical, causal, spatiotemporal, and often scarce. Calls for scientific priors and constraints, robust UQ, architectures handling multi-modality, and tight integration with classical scientific tools.

### 4. Advances in Financial AI (2nd Edition)

Building on last year's workshop. Focus on emergence of agentic systems in finance and the imperative of responsibility (ethics, fairness, accountability, transparency, robustness, regulation). Bringing together researchers, practitioners, and policymakers.

### 5. MLGenX (3rd Edition)

From Reasoning to Experimentation: Closing the Loop Between AI Agents and the Biological Lab. Focus on adaptive, interpretable, and experiment-aware AI systems that learn from feedback and drive biological insight. Charting the path toward lab-in-the-loop science and accelerating innovation in biology and drug discovery.

### 6. ReALM-GEN

Adapting and steering pretrained flow- and diffusion-based models toward real-world constraint satisfaction and alignment with user preferences. Bringing together researchers spanning theoretical foundations of ML and generative models, vision, language, robotics, and scientific applications.

### 7. Negative Results on LLMs

Venue for: (i) rigorous studies that demonstrate and analyze LLM limitations (leak-resistant reasoning probes, alignment stress tests, failure audits), and (ii) attempts on well-established ideas that did not deliver expected gains, with analyses that identify failure modes, boundary conditions, and lessons learned. Diagnostics, replications, counterfactual evaluations, and ablations. Converting setbacks into robust principles.

### 8. World Models (2nd Edition)

Scalable frameworks unifying generative modeling, sequential decision-making, multimodal learning, and causal reasoning. Three core themes: (i) understanding and knowledge extraction, (ii) large-scale training and evaluation, (iii) cross-modal and control-centric scaling. Building on 2025 workshop with 1,500+ participants. New: systems-level discussions, robotics case studies, failure-mode post-mortems. Culminating in a synthesis article.

### 9. Post-Training at Scale

Establishing rigorous and scalable methodologies for post-training (SFT, RL). A scaling laws mindset for post-training is largely absent. Catalyzing systematic understanding of how post-training scales across algorithms, data regimes, infrastructure, and objectives. Bringing together academic and industrial researchers.

### 10. Deep Generative Models (DeLTa 2026, 2nd Edition)

Bridging theory and practice in modern generative modeling. Two central questions: (1) unified theoretical frameworks for advanced generative models, (2) improving efficiency, reliability, and transferability. Expanded scope: flow matching, stochastic control, discrete/low-dimensional diffusion models, post-training theory, large language diffusion models. Short-paper track for early-career researchers.

### 11. Learning Meaningful Representations (LMRL)

Identifying key bottlenecks in development of virtual cells — in silico representations of a cell's behavior and dynamics. Four core ingredients: causality in biological systems, generative modeling, interpretable representations, and leveraging virtual cells for real-world impact. Bringing together the AIxBio community.

### 12. Hallucination in Agentic AI

Can we count on AI agents to get it right? Foundation models remain prone to hallucination — factually incorrect, semantically implausible, or detached from reality outputs. Challenges are amplified in agentic settings, where models execute sequences of decisions without continuous human oversight.

### 13. Test-Time Updates

Test-time updates (TTU) including test-time adaptation (TTA), post-training editing, in-context learning, and online continual learning. Relevant across model size: editing knowledge in large foundation models and adapting models on edge devices. Spanning robustness, customization, and computational efficiency. Methods, theory, systems, and evaluations across modalities, scales, and openness.

### 14. Re-Align

Representational alignment among artificial and biological neural systems. 688 papers submitted to ICLR 2026 on these topics (51% yearly increase). Two novel focuses: enabling neural control via representational alignment and evaluating downstream behaviors enabled by representational alignment.

### 15. Multi-Agent Learning and Generative AI

Growing convergence between multi-agent learning and generative AI. Three fronts: (1) LLM-based multi-agent systems in structured settings, (2) real-world distributed system control for smart cities, (3) human-AI interaction with richer modeling of human preferences, values, and behaviors. Bridging ML, game theory, cognitive science, and HCI.

### 16. Efficient Spatial Reasoning

Spatial reasoning with large foundation models: symbolic-neural integration, geometric deep learning, scalable reasoning architectures, and evaluation frameworks. Examining efficiency-accuracy trade-offs, cross-modal reasoning, and real-world robustness. Fostering collaboration across AI, cognitive science, and applied domains.

### 17. Generative ML for Biomolecular Design (3rd Edition)

Engineering proteins, ligands, nucleic acids, and cells. Bridging computationalists and experimentalists. Partnered with Nature Biotechnology for a special collection. Dedicated tracks for in-silico ML and hybrid ML-experimental biology research. Seed-grant competition for experimentalist-computationalist pairs. Wet-lab challenge sponsored by Adaptyv Bio.

### 18. ML for Remote Sensing (ML4RS, 4th Edition)

Theme: "From Publication to Practice." Bridging research innovations with real-world deployment. New: interactive tutorials track and journal proceedings for research track papers. Debate: "Foundation Models in ML4RS: Are We There Yet?" Emphasizing local engagement with Brazil's remote sensing and ML communities.

### 19. Latent & Implicit Thinking (LIT)

Going beyond Chain-of-Thought reasoning. Exploring how models reason within hidden representations or parameter space using continuous latent states, recurrent or looped architectures, and non-autoregressive formulations. Topics: latent-space reasoning tokens, looped and recurrent architectures, latent generative paradigms, theoretical insights on reasoning depth and efficiency.

### 20. Memory for Agentic Systems

The memory layer for LLM-based agents. Long-lived, safe, and useful agents require a principled memory substrate supporting single-shot learning of instances, context-aware retrieval, and consolidation into generalizable knowledge. Convening interdisciplinary researchers across RL, memory research, LLMs, agentic systems, and neuroscience.
